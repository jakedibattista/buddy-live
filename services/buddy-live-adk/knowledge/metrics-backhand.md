# Backhand scoring metrics

Eight metrics scored on every backhand rep. All 0–10; 7+ is good.

## weight transfer

**What it means:** shifting weight from back foot to front foot through
the puck. Drives lower-body power into the shot.

**Good looks like:** weight finishes on the front foot at follow-through.

**Fix cue:** "Drive your weight from back foot to front foot through
the puck."

**Recommended drill:** wall-shoot weight-transfer drill. Search hint:
"hockey weight transfer drill."

## posture and balance

**What it means:** staying tall through the shot. Leaning back kills
power and accuracy.

**Good looks like:** spine straight through release, no rocking back.

**Fix cue:** "Stay tall through the shot — don't lean back as you
release."

**Recommended drill:** backhand balance drill. Shoot from a narrow
stance to challenge balance. Search hint: "hockey backhand balance
posture drill."

## bottom hand

**What it means:** the bottom hand drives forward through the puck on
the backhand — same engine role as the wristshot, opposite direction.

**Good looks like:** bottom hand visibly drives forward through release.

**Fix cue:** "Drive forward with your bottom hand through the puck on
the backhand."

**Recommended drill:** bottom hand pull-through drill. Search hint:
"hockey bottom hand drill."

## extension through release

**What it means:** pushing the hands up and across the body after the
puck leaves the blade. Full extension = full power and lift.

**Good looks like:** hands finish high across the body, blade pointing
at the upper corner.

**Fix cue:** "Push your hands up and across your body after release —
full extension."

**Recommended drill:** backhand follow-through drill. Aim at the top
of a target. Search hint: "hockey backhand follow through drill."

## puck starting position

**What it means:** the puck starts off the back heel of the blade —
not in the middle or the toe.

**Good looks like:** puck visibly on the heel before the sweep starts.

**Fix cue:** "Start the puck on the back heel of your blade so you can
roll it forward."

**Recommended drill:** puck-in-the-pocket drill (backhand version).
Practice setting up with the puck on the heel. Search hint: "hockey
puck position backhand drill."

## puck control roll

**What it means:** the puck rolling from the back of the blade to the
front as the player sweeps through. This rolling motion adds spin and
keeps the puck on the blade longer for accuracy.

**Good looks like:** puck visibly rolls heel-to-toe along the blade
during the sweep.

**Fix cue:** "Roll the puck from the back of the blade to the front as
you sweep."

**Recommended drill:** backhand cup-and-roll drill. Slow-motion sweeps
focusing on the heel-to-toe roll. Search hint: "hockey backhand cup
roll drill."

## top hand control

**What it means:** keeping the top hand away from the body so the
stick has room to travel. Top hand glued to the body = no power.

**Good looks like:** clear gap between the top hand and the chest
through the sweep.

**Fix cue:** "Keep your top hand away from your body so the stick can
travel freely."

**Recommended drill:** backhand top hand drill. Hold a tennis ball
between top hand and chest to break the habit. Search hint: "hockey
backhand top hand drill."

## blade angle

**What it means:** how closed/open the blade face is at release. A
closed blade lifts the puck for the upper corners; open = floaters.

**Good looks like:** blade clearly cupped (closed) at release.

**Fix cue:** "Cup the puck on release — closed blade lifts it for the
upper corners."

**Recommended drill:** backhand blade angle drill. Aim for the top
corners specifically. Search hint: "hockey backhand blade angle drill."
