# Wristshot scoring metrics

Eight metrics scored on every wristshot rep. All scored 0–10; 7+ is
good. The coach shares ONE strength + ONE weakest metric per rep.

## front knee bend

**What it means:** how deeply the front knee bends through the shot.
Front knee bend gets the player low and engages the lower body — the
engine of every shot. Standing too tall = arms-only shot = no power.

**Good looks like:** front knee clearly bent past 90 degrees through
release, head over the puck.

**Fix cue:** "Get lower. Imagine sitting back into a chair before the
release."

**Recommended drill:** deep knee bend wristshots. 10 reps focusing only
on knee bend. Search hint: "hockey knee bend shooting drill."

## weight transfer

**What it means:** shifting weight from back foot to front foot through
the puck. Drives power into the shot via the legs, not just the arms.

**Good looks like:** clearly visible foot-to-foot weight shift, finishing
with weight on the front foot at follow-through.

**Fix cue:** "Drive your weight from back foot to front foot through the
puck."

**Recommended drill:** wall-shoot weight-transfer drill. Stand sideways
to a wall, focus on transferring weight as you shoot. Search hint:
"hockey weight transfer drill."

## back leg push

**What it means:** snapping the back leg straight as power is generated.
Like a coiled spring releasing.

**Good looks like:** back leg clearly extends straight through release.

**Fix cue:** "Snap your back leg straight as you release — like a coiled
spring."

**Recommended drill:** back-leg explosion drill. 10 reps with extra
emphasis on driving the back leg through. Search hint: "hockey back leg
push drill."

## bottom hand

**What it means:** the bottom hand pulls hard toward the body on release.
Generates wrist-snap power.

**Good looks like:** bottom hand visibly pulls toward the hip at release.

**Fix cue:** "Pull hard with your bottom hand toward your hip on release."

**Recommended drill:** bottom hand pull-through drill. Search hint:
"hockey bottom hand drill."

## top hand

**What it means:** the top hand rolls the wrist over at the very end —
the "snap" of the wristshot.

**Good looks like:** clearly visible wrist roll at the moment of release.

**Fix cue:** "Roll your top wrist over at the very end — that's the snap."

**Recommended drill:** top hand snap drill. Search hint: "hockey top
hand wrist snap drill."

## puck position at contact

**What it means:** where the puck is on the blade when the player
releases. Off the front foot is ideal — too far back = no power,
too far forward = no time to load the stick.

**Good looks like:** puck released off the front foot, not the back.

**Fix cue:** "Make contact off your front foot — not your back foot."

**Recommended drill:** puck contact-point drill. Place a marker, shoot
from the same spot every rep. Search hint: "hockey puck contact point
shooting drill."

## stick flex

**What it means:** the stick bending under load before release. The
flex is the engine — without it, the shot is arms-only.

**Good looks like:** visible bend in the shaft before the puck leaves
the blade.

**Fix cue:** "Push down into the floor to load the stick before the
snap."

**Recommended drill:** stick-load drill. Practice pushing into the
floor before release with no puck. Search hint: "hockey stick flex
loading drill."

## stance

**What it means:** the base position — feet, knees, head. Everything
flows from a good stance.

**Good looks like:** feet shoulder-width, knees bent, head over the
puck.

**Fix cue:** "Shoulder-width feet, knees bent, head over the puck."

**Recommended drill:** athletic stance basics. Hold the stance for 30
seconds before shooting. Search hint: "hockey shooting stance drill."
