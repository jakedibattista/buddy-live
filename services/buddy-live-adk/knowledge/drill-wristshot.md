# Wristshot

The quick-release shot. No big windup. Power comes from loading the stick
into the floor and snapping the wrists at the very end. The wristshot is
the most-used shot in hockey and the one young players should learn first.

## Key technique points

1. **Stance:** feet shoulder-width, knees bent, head over the puck.
2. **Puck position at start:** in the "pocket" near the back foot.
3. **Stick load:** push down into the floor so the stick flexes — the
   flex is the engine.
4. **Weight transfer:** drive from the back foot to the front foot through
   the puck.
5. **Bottom hand pull:** pull hard with the bottom hand toward the hip.
6. **Top hand snap:** roll the top wrist over at the very end. That's the
   "snap" that gives it speed.
7. **Follow through:** point the blade where you want the puck to go.

## Kid-friendly cheat sheet (10–13 yo)

"Knees bent like sitting in a chair. Puck starts by your back foot.
Push the stick into the floor as you swing — the stick should bend.
Shift your weight to your front foot through the puck. Pull with your
bottom hand, snap your top wrist. Point the blade where you want it to go."

## Common mistakes

- Standing too tall (no knee bend) → no power, shot goes weak.
- Puck too far forward at the start → no pocket, no time to load the stick.
- Pushing instead of snapping → the puck floats instead of zipping.
- Forgetting to transfer weight → arms-only shot, easy for the goalie.

## Recommended drill: stationary wristshots

5 reps slow, focusing on knee bend and weight transfer. Then 5 reps at
full speed. Search hint: "USA Hockey wristshot stationary drill."
